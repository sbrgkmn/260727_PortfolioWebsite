Calyx
Calyx is a public sculpture commissioned by the University of Roch-
ester and selected through a national competition RFP . Conceived as 
a campus landmark, the project brings together public art, computa-
tional design, material innovation, and digital fabrication. The work 
draws inspiration from leaf venation systems, where branching net-
works organize growth, distribute forces, and create resilient natural 
structures. Rather than visually imitating nature, Calyx translates 
biological organization into a generative design process. 
 
The sculpture was developed using custom leaf venation algorithms 
that generated three-dimensional branching patterns. These algo-
rithms simulated how veins grow, connect, thicken, and stabilize 
across a surface, producing an open lattice that rises from a compact 
base and expands outward into a canopy-like form. A geometry op-
timization protocol then translated the complex branching network 
into individual stainless steel pieces that could be fabricated from 
flat sheets. This process transformed digital pattern logic into cutta-
ble, weldable, and boltable plate assemblies. 
 
The final work was fabricated from optimized stainless steel plates 
and assembled through welded and bolted connections. Its open 
lattice allows light, views, and movement to pass through, while 
its vivid presence creates a recognizable campus landmark. Calyx 
demonstrates how generative algorithms, geometric optimization, 
and digital fabrication can converge in a realized architectural-scale 
public artwork.
22Computation
“Calyx” is a distinctive sculpture commissioned by 
the University of  Rochester to usher in an era where 
technology, art, nature, and knowledge seamlessly blend, 
sparking inspiration for future generations. Drawing its 
design inspiration from the intricate venation patterns 
of  leaves, which naturally form structural networks and 
organic shapes, “Calyx” serves dual purposes. It not 
only stands as an iconic landmark guiding visitors but 
also beckons students, offering them a communal space 
for relaxation and gathering.
The sculpture’s form emerges from specialized computer 
software that mimics the self-organization found in 
natural vein systems. Beginning at its base, simulated 
veins reach upwards, intertwining and supporting one 
another to establish structural joints and achieve a stable 
final shape. Preliminary structural analysis during the 
project’s early stages revealed patterns reminiscent of  
leaf  venation, characterized by hierarchical variations in 
material thickness. Leveraging these insights, “Calyx” 
was meticulously crafted from optimized stainless steel 
plates, seamlessly joined through welding and bolted 
connections.
