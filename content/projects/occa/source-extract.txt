The project situated within the contrasting historical 
context of  the museum, takes its inspiration after Ferrea 
Occa, a type of  Glass Sponge, which creates four-
legged intricate hollow surfaces. Considered by some 
experts to be the longest living form in the world, these 
Hexactinellid sponges were studied and documented 
in 1904 by the German biologist Ernst Haeckel in his 
Artistic Forms of  Nature. The design form combines 
two hedron geometries resembling sponges that are 
articulated by branches facing in different directions. 
The produced surface geometry is built by riveting 
black and white stripes running in opposite directions 
before being hanged from four points combined with 
steel ropes.
This installation project is the culmination of  “Parametric 
Design: Minimal Tectonics” seminar completed during 
Spring 2022 academic term of  Architecture program 
where research is completed in three stages. In the first 
stage, students conducted  “form-finding” experiments 
using the soap film technique developed by Frei Otto 
in the 1960s and produced different minimal surfaces. 
In the second stage, the produced geometries were 
replicated and varied using parametric modeling tools. 
At the last stage, the selected design was fabricated 
using digital fabrication algorithms and produced from 
laser cut forex sheets and rivet joints.

Occa
Occa is a suspended installation developed for Rezan Has Museum 
in Istanbul as the culmination of the Spring 2022 Parametric De-
sign: Minimal Tectonics seminar. The project explores the transla-
tion of biological form, computational geometry, and digital fabrica-
tion into an architectural-scale installation. It takes inspiration from 
Ferrea Occa, a type of glass sponge documented by Ernst Haeckel in 
Artistic Forms of Nature. Rather than reproducing the sponge liter-
ally, the project abstracts its hollow structure, porous organization, 
and skeletal logic into a lightweight spatial system. 
 
The design process moved through analog and digital stages. Stu-
dents first conducted soap-film form-finding experiments inspired 
by Frei Otto’s minimal surface studies, using surface tension, curva-
ture, and boundary conditions as generative forces. These physical 
studies were then translated into parametric models, where the 
geometries were replicated, varied, and refined through computa-
tional control. 
 
The final installation was produced from laser-cut forex sheet com-
ponents, joined with rivets, and suspended from four points with 
steel ropes. Its black and white striped components emphasize cur-
vature, directionality, and depth, making the piece legible as both 
patterned surface and volumetric form. Occa demonstrates how 
computational design pedagogy can transform natural precedent, 
material experimentation, and digital fabrication into public-facing 
creative work.
